What you need

Five small things. Nothing here is specialist — a Raspberry Pi is a $45 computer the size of a credit card, sold everywhere. Pick one of the options below; they are all the same project, just different budgets and board choices.

Things you almost certainly already have

A computer

Windows, Mac or Linux — used once, to write the operating system onto the memory card.

Your router's password

Usually printed on a sticker on the router itself. You need it for one setting.

A free socket near the router

The Pi draws about as much power as a phone charger and lives there permanently.

Wi-Fi or a spare LAN port

Wired is better and more reliable, but Wi-Fi genuinely works fine.

The four mistakes that cost people an afternoon

A phone charger instead of a real power supply. The Pi needs a steady 5 V at 3 A. Underpowered Pis behave strangely in ways that look like software bugs: random freezes, corrupted cards, Wi-Fi dropping.
A cheap no-name memory card. The Pi writes constantly. Buy a branded A2-rated card (SanDisk, Samsung) — $10 instead of $4, and it lasts years instead of months.
The wrong case for the board. Pi 4, Pi 5 and Pi Zero are all different shapes. Check the case lists your exact model.
Buying a Pi 5 with 8 GB “to be safe”. This software uses under 100 MB of memory. Spend the difference on a good power supply and a proper card.
